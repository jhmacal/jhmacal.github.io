Reference Catalog

Interactables Glossary

Named UI patterns for use across jhmacal.com articles. Each pattern carries a fixed name, a use-case, and a working demo. Ask for any of them by name in future builds.

Paged Carousel

PAGED

A stack of cards the reader pages through with gold arrows. State is independent if multiple sit on the page. Earns its keep when the items are too many to show at once and each one needs its own attention. Already used: LIVE and POSTPONED in the Omnibus article.

DEMO Three obligations
Article 4
AI literacy

Providers and deployers must ensure their staff have sufficient understanding of the AI systems they use.

Live since 2 February 2025
Article 5
Prohibitions

Banned practices including social scoring, untargeted facial scraping, and manipulative or exploitative systems.

Live since 2 February 2025
Article 50
Transparency

Deployers of deepfake systems must disclose that content has been artificially generated or manipulated.

Live from 2 August 2026
1 / 3

Reference name: Paged Carousel. Variant: independent state (two on one page).

Quadrant Chart with Axes

2x2

Four items plotted across two named axes. Earns its keep only when the two axes are real and the four items decompose cleanly across them. Already used: the four governance moves in the Omnibus article.

Reference name: Quadrant Chart with Axes. The axes must be earned, not decorative.

Interactive Node Timeline

CHRONOLOGY

A row of dated nodes on a horizontal line. Click a node, the detail panel below updates. Earns its keep for chronological argument where each beat carries weight. Already used: the Seven Moves card in the Omnibus article.

First milestone

Each step gets its own panel of substantive content. The reader clicks to advance through the chronology at their own pace.

Reference name: Interactive Node Timeline. Sized for 5 to 8 nodes; more than that, switch to Paged Carousel.

Blockquote Pull

QUOTATION

A short verbatim quotation, set in italic serif with a vertical gold rule on the left. Earns its keep when the exact words of the source matter more than paraphrase. Already used: Article 4 verbatim in the Omnibus article.

Providers and deployers of AI systems shall take measures to ensure (…) AI literacy of their staff and other persons dealing with the operation and use of AI systems on their behalf (…)

Reference name: Blockquote Pull. Keep short. If the quote runs long, paraphrase and footnote.

Numbered Editorial List

PROSE-NATIVE

Display numerals in Cormorant Garamond gold, paired with a heading and a paragraph. No card borders. The most under-used and most prose-native pattern available. Earns its keep when the page already has too many UI components and the four items need to read as editorial prose.

01

First move

A paragraph of substantive content. Reads as part of the article, not a UI grid sitting on top of the page. Particularly strong when the items have logical sequence rather than spatial layout.

02

Second move

A paragraph of substantive content. The numerals carry the structural weight without demanding visual real estate the way cards do.

03

Third move

A paragraph of substantive content. The pattern scales to as many items as needed without breaking the visual rhythm.

Reference name: Numbered Editorial List. Default when the page is already dense.

Tabs

SWITCH

A bar of clickable headings; only the active panel shows. Earns its keep when items are siblings of the same kind and only one can be read at a time. Cheaper to navigate than carousels because all options are visible at once.

Provider

An entity that develops or places an AI system on the market under its own name or trademark.

Deployer

An entity using an AI system under its authority, except where the AI system is used in the course of a personal non-professional activity.

Controller

Per GDPR, the natural or legal person determining the purposes and means of processing personal data.

Reference name: Tabs. Use for 2 to 5 sibling categories.

DISCLOSURE

A list of rows; click a row to expand. Earns its keep for FAQ-style content where the reader picks which to read deeply. Quieter visually than tabs.

Inline Definitions

HOVER

Defined terms in the prose carry a subtle dotted gold underline. Hover to see the one-sentence operational definition in a small tooltip. Earns its keep when the audience mix is part lawyer and part operator, and you want to define without breaking the reading rhythm.

The Act regulates Annex III standalone uses, Annex I product-embedded uses, and GPAI models. The fines under Article 99 are the highest in the EU regulatory stack.

Reference name: Inline Definitions. Each tooltip ≤ 280 chars.

Hover Reveal Card

PROGRESSIVE

A grid of cards showing only titles until hovered. Hover swaps title for body. Earns its keep when titles alone tell the whole story but readers benefit from optional depth. Avoid on mobile-first audiences (no hover state).

Inventory

A register of AI systems with owner, use case, status, data provenance, and risk tier.

Mapping

Allocation of roles across provider, deployer, controller, processor, and downstream operator.

Literacy

Role-specific training before access, with completion evidence retained and refreshed.

Reference name: Hover Reveal Card. Always pair with a no-hover fallback for mobile.

Typographic Block

ASIDE

A single bordered block (left rule in gold) containing labeled sub-paragraphs. Reads as a designed aside or editorial sidebar rather than a UI grid. Earns its keep when the four items are conceptually a single argument with named beats.

Reference name: Typographic Block. The quietest of the four-item patterns.

A dedicated question-and-answer block for reader objections, implementation details, or recurring clarifications. It overlaps with accordion mechanics but carries a different editorial promise.
