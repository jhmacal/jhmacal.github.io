Legal AI Daily Lesson 1

Legal AI Is Not One Thing

Most lawyers feel disoriented because every conversation uses one word for four different things. A model, a tool, a workflow, and governance are not the same object. Once you can separate them, vendor demos, firm policy, client questions, and internal adoption meetings become easier to read.

The Situation

The room says AI. You need to ask which layer.

A partner says the firm is testing AI for contract review. A business client asks whether legal can use AI to summarize incoming questions. A vendor says its platform has a legal reasoning engine. Each sentence sounds like it describes one thing. It does not.

The practical lawyer move is to slow the room down and ask: are we discussing the model, the tool, the workflow, or the governance around the work?

Core Concept

Legal AI usually means a stack of decisions. The model generates or classifies language. The tool packages that model into something lawyers can use. The workflow decides where the output goes. Governance decides whether the whole thing can happen safely.

Why It Matters
01

You stop asking blurry questions.

“Is the AI safe?” is too broad. “What data reaches the model?” and “Who reviews the output before use?” are questions the room can answer.

02

You hear vendor demos differently.

A beautiful product screen can hide weak governance. A strong model can still sit inside a poor workflow. The layers keep you from being impressed by the wrong thing.

03

You protect legal judgment.

Lawyers remain responsible for competence, confidentiality, supervision, and judgment. The stack shows where that responsibility has to re-enter the process.

Model

The model produces language or classifications.

A model is the underlying system that generates, classifies, summarizes, or transforms text. It does not decide your matter workflow. It does not know your risk tolerance. It responds to inputs.

How It Shows Up

Day-to-day example

A lawyer asks a tool to summarize a draft contract. The model generates the summary. The tool provides the interface. The workflow decides whether the summary goes into a matter note. Governance decides whether client information can be entered and who must review the result.

Law firm lens

A firm pilot can fail if the policy only approves a tool name. The better approval defines permitted matters, user group, data limits, review obligations, and how output can reach a client-facing document.

In-house lens

A legal department can move faster when business users know which intake questions can use automation and which answers still require a lawyer. The workflow matters as much as the model.
