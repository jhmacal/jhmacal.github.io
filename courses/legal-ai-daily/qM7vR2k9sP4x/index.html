Legal AI Daily Lesson 1

Legal AI Is Not One Thing

Legal AI enters the day as requests, tools, workflows, and records. The first lawyer move is to name which layer is actually moving.

The Situation

AI can mean many things.

Monday afternoon, the work arrives in three different forms.

Practice meeting An adjacent group is piloting AI for contract review.
Client call The client asks for an AI summary of a hundred-page policy.
Platform note The tool everyone uses now advertises a legal reasoning engine.

Together, those moments point to the stack: model, tool, workflow, and governance record.

Core Concept
Model Generates, classifies, summarizes, or transforms language.
Tool Turns that capability into the surface people use.
Workflow Decides where output goes and when counsel returns.
Governance Creates the limits, review duty, logging, and proof.
Why It Matters
01

The model performs a bounded language task.

Ask what information reaches it, what task it performs, and where the answer starts to sound like legal judgment.

02

The tool turns hidden architecture into a screen.

Ask what sits underneath the demo: retention, access, retrieval, logs, privilege boundaries, and vendor promises.

03

Governance preserves the evidence story.

Ask what legal allowed, who reviewed the output, and what record will explain the decision later.

Interactive Lab

Separate the stack.

Inspect all four layers before moving on. The approval analysis changes with the layer.

Model

The model performs the language task.

A model generates, classifies, summarizes, compares, or transforms language based on the input it receives and the surrounding instructions it can see. The legal question is what information reaches it, what task it has been asked to perform, what uncertainty remains in the answer, and whether the user can recognize when the output has crossed from language support into legal judgment.

How It Shows Up

Day-to-day example

The client sends a long policy and asks for a same-day summary. Legal has to decide what can enter, who reviews, and what record survives.

Law firm lens

A tool approval needs matter types, eligible users, data limits, privilege treatment, client rules, and review path.

In-house lens

A legal team moves faster when business users know which answers stay internal and which ones require signoff.
